### Wireless Network Topology

Wireless networks are set up in one of three ways:
* [[#^4e8a1c|Infrastructure Mode]]
* [[#^b6f03d|Ad Hoc Mode]]
* [[#^1d9c7e|Wireless Mesh Topology]]

Infrastructure Mode is the most common type of wireless network. Ad Hoc Mode is decentralized, and does not require a router or access point.

A Wireless Mesh can use different radio frequencies combined into one network, like:
* Bluetooth
* Wi-Fi
* Microwave
* Satellite
* Cellular

Two or three or four different topologies are combined to create a wireless mesh network, making it very redundant. It is most commonly used during disaster relief. Ex. Basecamp that has Wi-Fi in it, but then it is also connected to another base camp via microwave link. That base camp may be then connected back to their home country via satellite.

### Glossary

Infrastructure Mode: The most common type of wireless network that uses a wireless access point as a centralized point. ^4e8a1c

Ad Hoc Mode: Decentralized wireless network which creates Peer-to-Peer connections and does not require a router or access point. ^b6f03d

Wireless Mesh Topology: An interconnection of different types of nodes, devices, and radios. ^1d9c7e
